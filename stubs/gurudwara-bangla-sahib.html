Gurudwara Bangla Sahib

State: Delhi | Category: spiritual | Best Time: Oct – Mar

Gurudwara Bangla Sahib was originally a bungalow (haveli) belonging to Raja Jai Singh of Amber (Jaipur) in the 17th century, known as Jaisinghpura Palace. The eighth Sikh Guru, Guru Har Krishan Ji, resided here during his stay in Delhi in 1664 when smallpox and cholera epidemics broke out. The Guru gave clean water from the well in the haveli to the sick, which cured numerous sufferers. Today, the pristine sarovar (holy pool), gleaming golden dome, giant 24/7 Langar hall feeding tens of thousands daily, and tranquil marble parikrama make it a world-renowned spiritual haven of selfless service and peace.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Sarovar (Holy Healing Pool) — The sanctified freshwater sarovar whose water ('Amrit') is revered for healing properties, dating back to Guru Har Krishan Ji's relief work in 1664.
  • Main Sanctum & Golden Dome — The dazzling white marble sanctum crowned with an intricately gilded pure gold dome where continuous live Gurbani Kirtan echoes day and night.
  • Guru Harkrishan Langar Hall — One of the world's most impressive community kitchens, serving wholesome free vegetarian meals cooked with devotion to over 35,000 visitors every day.
  • Connaught Place Inner Circle & Central Park — The celebrated colonial Georgian colonnade and vibrant pedestrian market plaza dotted with top international cafes, flagship boutiques, and Central Park.
  • Jantar Mantar (New Delhi) — The UNESCO-recognized 18th-century astronomical observatory built by Maharaja Jai Singh II of Jaipur, featuring monumental terracotta-red geometric masonry instruments.
  • Agrasen ki Baoli — A protected 14th-century architectural stepwell on Hailey Road featuring 108 steep stone steps descending into the earth, lined with arched alcoves and centuries of Delhi lore.
