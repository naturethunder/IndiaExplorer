Delhi

State: Delhi | Category: heritage | Best Time: Oct – Mar

From the 12th-century soaring minaret at Qutub Minar and the proto-Taj Mughal symmetry of Humayun’s Tomb to the towering red sandstone battlements of Red Fort and the bustling medieval alleys of Chandni Chowk, Delhi is a living museum of empires. Built upon the sacred banks of the Yamuna River, it boasts three UNESCO World Heritage Sites, India's largest congregational mosque at Jama Masjid, the mystical ruins of Hauz Khas, and the spiritual grandeur of Akshardham.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Red Fort (Lal Qila) — Monumental 17th-century red sandstone fortress of the Mughal dynasty and venue of the national Independence Day address.
  • Qutub Minar Complex (UNESCO) — The world’s tallest brick minaret standing 72.5m tall alongside the 4th-century rust-resistant Iron Pillar and Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque.
  • Humayun’s Tomb (UNESCO) — Magnificent garden-tomb of the second Mughal emperor that set the architectural precedent for the Taj Mahal.
  • Jama Masjid — One of the largest mosques in India built by Shah Jahan in 1656, featuring two 40-metre minarets and a courtyard holding 25,000 worshippers.
  • Chandni Chowk & Khari Baoli — Vibrant 370-year-old market avenue famous for street cuisine (Paranthe Wali Gali) and Asia’s largest wholesale spice market at Khari Baoli.
  • Swaminarayan Akshardham Temple — Colossal stone-carved Hindu temple complex showcasing 10,000 years of Indian culture, boat rides, and the Sahaj Anand water show.
