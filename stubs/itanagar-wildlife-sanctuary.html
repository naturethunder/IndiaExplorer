Itanagar Wildlife Sanctuary

State: Arunachal Pradesh | Category: wildlife | Best Time: Mar – Jun, Sep – Nov

Nestled at the foothills of the Eastern Himalayas, the Itanagar Wildlife Sanctuary spans over 140 square kilometers of pristine subtropical wet hill forests, bamboo groves, and evergreen canopies. Home to diverse wildlife including clouded leopards, capped langurs, and over 200 bird species, the sanctuary encompasses the tranquil emerald waters of Gyakar Sinyi (Ganga Lake) and the ancient 14th-century brick ramparts of Ita Fort.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Ganga Lake (Gyakar Sinyi) — Gyakar Sinyi, popularly known as Ganga Lake, is a tranquil natural freshwater lake nestled within the verdant subtropical forests of the sanctuary, framed by ancient trees, wild orchids, and bamboo groves.
  • Itanagar — Itanagar () is the capital and largest town of the Indian state of Arunachal Pradesh. The seat of Arunachal Pradesh Legislative Assembly, the seat of government of Arunachal Pradesh, and the seat of Gauhati High Court permanent bench at Naharlagun are all in Itanagar.
  • Ita Fort — Ita Fort in Itanagar town is an important historical site in the state of Arunachal Pradesh, India. The name literally means "Fort of Bricks" (brick being called "Ita" in the Assamese language).
  • Diocese of Itanagar — The Roman Catholic Diocese of Itanagar (Dioecesis Itanagarensis) in India was created on 7 December 2005 by splitting it from the Diocese of Tezpur. It is a suffragan diocese of the Archdiocese of Guwahati.
  • Raj Bhavan (Lok Bhavan) — Perched atop a picturesque hilltop in Itanagar, Raj Bhavan is the official residence of the Governor of Arunachal Pradesh, renowned for its landscaped gardens and sweeping views of the green capital valley.
  • Aalo (Along) River Valley — Situated along the sparkling waters of the Siyom River, the scenic Aalo valley features hanging bamboo bridges, orange orchards, and vibrant Adi tribal culture amidst verdant hill ranges.
