Bhimtal

State: Uttarakhand | Category: hill_station | Best Time: Mar – Jun & Sep – Dec

Bhimtal is an idyllic lake town perched at an elevation of 1,370 meters in the Nainital district of Uttarakhand. Significantly larger and far more peaceful than neighboring Naini Lake, Bhimtal is named after Bhima from the Mahabharata epic, who according to legend created the freshwater lake by striking the earth with his mace. At the center of the lake lies a picturesque natural island housing an aquarium with exotic freshwater species, accessible only by rowboats. With its historic 1883 Victoria Dam, 17th-century Bhimeshwar Mahadev Temple, tranquil pine-scented air, and proximity to Sattal and Naukuchiatal, Bhimtal serves as the quintessential gateway to Uttarakhand's enchanting Lake District.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Bhimtal Lake & Central Island Aquarium — The crowning jewel of Bhimtal, this magnificent C-shaped freshwater lake is larger than Nainital. An iconic island at its center houses a thriving aquarium featuring rare fish species from Mexico and South Africa, accessible by scenic rowboats and pedal boats.
  • Bhimeshwar Mahadev Temple — A deeply revered 17th-century stone temple dedicated to Lord Shiva, constructed by Baz Bahadur, King of the Chand dynasty. Situated right by the lake embankment, legends say Pandava prince Bhima worshipped Shiva here during their exile.
  • Victoria Dam & Lakeside Promenade — Constructed in 1883 by British engineers, this 40-foot masonry dam at the southern corner of Bhimtal features beautifully manicured terraced flower gardens, scenic pedestrian walkways, and panoramic vistas of the lake and forested ridges.
  • Hidimba Parvat & Vankhandi Ashram — A verdant hillock rising 5 km from Bhimtal, named after Hidimba of the Mahabharata. It hosts the serene Vankhandi Ashram, founded by an environmentalist monk, surrounded by a designated wildlife sanctuary sanctuary home to barking deer and leopards.
  • Karkotak Temple Hill — Perched on a high ridge overlooking the Bhimtal valley, this temple honors Karkotak Nag, the legendary king of serpents. Thousands visit during Rishi Panchami. The hilltop offers sweeping 360-degree views of both Bhimtal and neighboring mountain ranges.
