Bela Church

State: Kerala | Category: spiritual | Best Time: Nov – Feb

Bela Church, formally designated as Our Lady of Sorrows Church, stands on a scenic hilltop in Bela near Badiadka, 15 km north of Kasaragod and 50 km from Mangaluru. Built in 1890 under the historic Roman Catholic Diocese of Mangalore, it is the oldest church in Kasaragod district. Acclaimed for its Gothic Revival stone architecture, ornate sanctuary, tall bell tower, and a unique 300-metre subterranean tunnel cave grotto, the church is a prominent pilgrimage landmark celebrated for its annual feast and rich centenary heritage.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Neerchal Cultural Heritage — Neerchal is a charming rural heritage village in Kasaragod district situated just 1 km from Bela Church. Known for its traditional agricultural landscapes, ancestral manors, and vibrant local cultural harmony.
  • Seethangoli & Naikap Hills — Seethangoli is an elevated semi-urban village connecting northern Kasaragod with the interior highlands. Surrounded by green valleys, historic shrines, and pleasant viewpoints overlooking arecanut plantations.
  • Ananthapura Lake Temple — Ananthapura Lake Temple is the only lake temple in Kerala, situated in the center of a serene rectangular lake in Kasaragod. Dating back to the 9th century, it is the original seat (Moolasthanam) of Anantha Padmanabha Swamy and famous for its legendary vegetarian lake crocodile.
  • Mallikarjuna Temple, Kasaragod — The historic Mallikarjuna Temple is one of the most prominent Shiva temples in Kasaragod town. Believed to have been consecrated by the legendary sage Ikshvaku, it is renowned for its annual festival and Yakshagana performances.
  • Kumbla Coastal Town & Estuary — Kumbla is a historic coastal port and former seat of the Kumbla Rajas along the Arabian Sea. It features scenic backwater estuaries, ancient bridges, and coastal village life fringed by coconut palm groves.
  • Chandragiri Fort & River Estuary — Built in the 17th century by Shivappa Nayaka of Bednore, Chandragiri Fort occupies a high vantage point overlooking the confluence of the Chandragiri River with the Arabian Sea. Its weathered laterite bastions offer panoramic sunset views.
