Taj Mahal

State: Uttar Pradesh | Category: heritage | Best Time: Oct – Mar

Commissioned in 1631 by the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan to house the tomb of his favourite wife, Mumtaz Mahal, the Taj Mahal is recognized worldwide as the jewel of Muslim art in India and one of the universally admired masterpieces of the world's heritage. Set within a vast 17-hectare Mughal Charbagh garden complex with reflecting pools, red sandstone mosques, and intricate marble pietra dura inlay, it stands as an enduring symbol of eternal love.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Agra Fort — A monumental 16th-century Mughal fortress of red sandstone, featuring Jahangiri Mahal, Diwan-i-Am, Diwan-i-Khas, and breathtaking views of the Taj Mahal across the Yamuna.
  • Mehtab Bagh — The historic charbagh Moonlight Garden situated perfectly across the Yamuna River, renowned as the ultimate sunset vantage point for reflection photos of the Taj Mahal.
  • Tomb of I'timād-ud-Daulah (Baby Taj) — Often described as a 'jewel box' and the precursor to the Taj Mahal, this exquisite white marble mausoleum features delicate jali latticework and floral pietra dura inlay.
  • Fatehpur Sikri — The fortified 16th-century Mughal capital founded by Emperor Akbar, home to the colossal Buland Darwaza, Jama Masjid, Panch Mahal, and the serene Tomb of Salim Chishti.
  • Akbar's Tomb, Sikandra — An architectural masterpiece combining red sandstone and white marble, set inside a vast 119-acre garden inhabited by peacocks and blackbucks.
  • Jama Masjid, Agra — A monumental 17th-century congregational mosque built by Jahanara Begum, showcasing graceful inverted dome architecture and red sandstone courtyards.
