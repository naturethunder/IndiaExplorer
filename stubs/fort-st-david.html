Fort St. David

State: Tamil Nadu | Category: heritage | Best Time: Nov – Feb

Fort St David, now in ruins, was a British fort in the town of Cuddalore, a hundred miles south of Chennai on the Coromandel Coast of India. It is located near silver beach without any maintenance. It was named for the patron saint of Wales because the governor of Madras at the time, Elihu Yale, was Welsh.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Silver Beach, Cuddalore — The second longest beach on the Coromandel Coast, boasting sweeping golden shores where the Gadilam River flows peacefully into the Bay of Bengal.
  • Devanathaswamy Temple, Thiruvanthipuram — Ancient 1,000-year-old Divya Desam temple dedicated to Lord Vishnu, dramatically positioned at the foot of the sacred medicinal Aushada Giri hill.
  • Padaleeswarar Temple — 7th-century Chola period Shiva temple celebrated in the Thevaram hymns, featuring colossal multi-tiered granite gopurams and carved pillared halls.
  • Gadilam River Estuary — Scenic estuarine waterway where the historic Gadilam River winds past the crumbling brick ramparts of Fort St. David into the Bay of Bengal.
  • Cuddalore Government Museum — Regional repository housing rare Pallava and Chola stone sculptures, ancient bronzes, ammonite fossils, and British East India Company colonial relics.
  • Pichavaram Mangrove Forest — The world’s second largest mangrove wetland system, comprising over 1,100 hectares of interconnected tidal canals fringed by dense root canopies.
