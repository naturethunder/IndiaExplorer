Sattal

State: Uttarakhand | Category: hill_station | Best Time: Mar – Jun & Oct – Dec

Sattal (meaning "Seven Lakes") is an interconnected cluster of seven pristine freshwater lakes nestled amidst dense oak, pine, and rhododendron forests in the Lower Himalayas at an altitude of 1,370 meters. The seven lakes—Ram Tal, Sita Tal, Lakshman Tal, Panna Tal, Nal-Damyanti Tal, Sukha Tal, and Garud Tal—are fed by underground springs and natural mountain runoffs. Renowned worldwide among ornithologists and nature photographers, Sattal is one of India's richest biodiversity hotspots, supporting over 500 species of resident and migratory Himalayan birds, 525 butterfly species, and over 11,000 insect varieties. Sattal offers an untouched, non-commercialized eco-paradise where visitors can immerse themselves in kayak paddling, woodland hiking, and silent meditation.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Ram, Sita & Lakshman Tal — The core interconnected trio of Sattal's seven freshwater lakes. Named after the figures of the Ramayana who are believed to have stayed here during exile, these pristine waters are surrounded by dense oak and pine woods and interconnected by natural channels.
  • Garud Tal (Panna Tal) — Separated from the main cluster by a forested ridge, Garud Tal is a solitary, emerald-green lake steeped in seclusion and mystery. Named after the divine eagle mount of Vishnu, it is surrounded by untouched wilderness and is a sanctuary for solitude seekers.
  • Nal-Damyanti Tal — A small, sacred lake situated 2 km before the main Sattal cluster. Surrounded by antique Kumaoni stone houses and terraced hills, tradition holds that the palace of King Nal and Queen Damyanti sank into its waters. Its shallow, clear waters are home to schools of Himalayan mahseer.
  • Sattal Christian Ashram & Forest Trails — Established in 1930 by E. Stanley Jones, a close associate of Mahatma Gandhi, this secluded ashram is set amidst 200 acres of undisturbed pine and oak forest. It remains an international center for interfaith meditation, Christian contemplation, and peaceful nature walks.
  • Subhash Dhara & Butterfly Sanctuary — A pristine freshwater spring cascading down a moss-covered cliff into the oak forest. Sattal is famous as one of India's premier butterfly hubs with over 525 species, and the nearby Butterfly Museum founded by Frederic Smetacek houses an extraordinary collection.
