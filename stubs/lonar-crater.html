Lonar Crater Lake

State: Maharashtra | Category: nature | Best Time: Nov – Mar

Lonar Crater Lake is a world-renowned National Geo-Heritage Monument located at Lonar in Buldhana district, Maharashtra. Created approximately 52,000 years ago by a hyper-velocity meteorite impact in basalt rock, it is the only known hyper-velocity impact crater in basalt anywhere on Earth. Spanning an almost circular diameter of 1.8 kilometres with steep 137-metre basalt rim cliffs, the emerald saline-alkaline lake is fringed by unique biodiversity, perennial mineral springs at Gomukh, and magnificent 11th-century Hemadpanthi stone temples including the Daitya Sudhan Temple.

Top Attractions & Places to Visit

  • Lonar Meteorite Impact Crater Basin — The world's only hyper-velocity impact crater formed in basalt rock, stretching 1.8 km across. Its highly saline-alkaline water supports unique microbial life and rare mineral formations.
  • Daitya Sudhan Temple — An extraordinary 11th-century Hemadpanthi Vishnu temple constructed in the shape of an asymmetrical star. The exterior walls are encrusted with exquisite stone sculptures depicting epics, celestial dancers, and Vishnu's avatars.
  • Gomukh Temple & Natural Spring — Situated on the upper rim of the crater, Gomukh Mandir features a perennial stream of fresh water emerging from a cow's mouth spout into a stone kalyani tank where pilgrims bathe before descending into the crater.
  • Kamalja Devi Temple — Located deep inside the crater basin beside the saltwater lake, this ancient temple dedicated to Kamalja Devi (an incarnation of Goddess Mahalakshmi) is said to be the spot where the demon Lavanasura was vanquished.
  • Amber Khana Temple Ruins — The stone ruins of Amber Khana, located on the southern lip of the crater, represent an impressive Hemadpanthi structure that once served as a royal granary and defensive watchpoint overlooking the entire lake.
  • Mothha Maruti Temple (Sleeping Hanuman) — Famous for its massive reclining monolith idol of Lord Hanuman sculpted from magnetic rock. Local belief holds that the stone of the idol possesses unusual magnetic properties tied to the ancient meteorite impact.
